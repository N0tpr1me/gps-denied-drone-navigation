# What drone engineers need from GPS-denied software (market research, Nov 2026)

Research note, 2026-11-01. Not a decision: anything here that would change a Decided row in
`docs/decisions.md` or the product strategy goes to Zain first. Sources are public (PX4 and
ArduPilot GitHub and forums, the ModalAI forum, PX4 Developer Summit abstracts, job posts,
vendor pages, regulator texts, trade press), read in early November 2026. The web-fetch budget
ran out part way through, so some sources were seen by title only; the text marks those, and
marks single anecdotes and vendor claims. The "what we have" side comes from this repository
at commit 8f5b9a8. Numbers about our own results live in `docs/baseline.md` and
`docs/route-follow.md`; the ones quoted here are copies for context.

## Headline: prove the safe fallback before adding features

Most senior engineers at PX4 and ArduPilot drone makers are not asking for "flight without GPS" as such. They want a drone that does not lurch, fly away or land blind when its position estimate jumps, resets or drops out, and that tells the pilot why. That is the failure posted most often in public forums from 2022 to 2026, and vendors such as ModalAI admit it in writing. Structure Hold is aimed straight at it. In PX4 software-in-the-loop simulation (SITL: real PX4 code flying a simulated drone in Gazebo), policy v22's 95th-percentile hold error (p95: the distance the drone stays inside 95 % of the time) was 54 to 55 % lower than a tuned stock PX4 over 8 seeds. When its classical watchdog trips, it hands back to a hover that a forward rangefinder keeps off the wall. But all of this is simulation on one simulated airframe, and nothing has flown. So the gaps that matter most are not new flight features. They are: tests that copy the exact failures engineers report, a ground-station warning when the watchdog trips, the failure paperwork their regulators ask for, tools to onboard a customer's own drone, and above all a first real flight on a partner's drone. Route Follow matches a real tunnel and sewer need, but it is months away from being a PX4 mode. The one item that is safe to start today, with no GPU server and no change to a Decided row, is a **field-failure test pack with ground-station trip warnings**. Its results then back a failure-mode table that buyers can paste into their own safety case. That table is an FMEA: each way the system can fail, how the failure is caught, what the drone does next, and the test that proves it.

## Estimate faults, not missing GPS, cause the crashes engineers report

The loudest and most dangerous problem is the position estimate jumping or resetting in flight. EKF2 is PX4's estimator: it fuses the sensors into one position. VIO (visual-inertial odometry) is a camera-plus-IMU position source. When EKF2 resets, PX4's position modes do not move their setpoints, so the drone makes **"dangerous (and false) corrections"**. That issue stayed open for four years ([PX4 #19830](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/issues/19830)). In 2026 a ModalAI engineer wrote that **"our PX4 flavour doesn't handle VIO resets gracefully always"** ([ModalAI forum 5333](https://forum.modalai.com/topic/5333/vio-problem)). An older thread reports VIO resetting to 0,0,0 mid-air and crashing, and another user lost 2 to 3 flights in 10 (a single anecdote from 2022) ([ModalAI forum 1383](https://forum.modalai.com/topic/1383/vio-coordinates-suddenly-resets-to-0-0-0-in-the-air-every-flight.md)). ArduPilot has the same class of problem. Source switching can jump the position, by about 300 m in one report, when GPS comes back ([ArduPilot #27729](https://github.com/ArduPilot/ardupilot/issues/27729)), and the umbrella fix has been open since 2020 ([ArduPilot #15859](https://github.com/ArduPilot/ardupilot/issues/15859)). The failsafes that follow are blunt: "blind land" ([PX4 #26911](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/issues/26911), [PX4 #28642](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/issues/28642)), a landing aimed at the origin ([PX4 #23773](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/issues/23773)), or a descent while still moving at about 5 m/s that hit the ground at an angle ([discuss.px4.io 42682](https://discuss.px4.io/t/crash-after-loss-of-gps/42682)).

The second pain is getting the estimate into EKF2 at all. Frames, yaw alignment, unknown delay and data that is silently rejected come up again and again. A PX4 change in 2026 stopped a VIO-only ModalAI drone from arming ([PX4 #27031](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/issues/27031)), and PX4 kept merging frame and yaw fixes through 2026 ([PX4 PR #27302](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/pull/27302), [PX4 PR #29327](https://github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/pull/29327)). One user had to patch PX4 just to log their VIO delay ([discuss.px4.io 39522](https://discuss.px4.io/t/how-to-fix-vio-latency/39522)). New releases also break working setups: after PX4 1.16 broke several flow sensors, forum advice was to stay on 1.15.4 ([discuss.px4.io 48559](https://discuss.px4.io/t/quad-drifts-randomly-in-position-hold-px4/48559)).

Wind near structures barely appears in the forums. The best direct analogue comes from ArduPilot's lead developer. Flying on optical flow in strong wind, **"the EKF is estimating the position correctly, it's the controller's that can't keep up"** ([ArduPilot #28242](https://github.com/ArduPilot/ardupilot/issues/28242)). I found no forum thread about holding next to a wall or under a deck in gusts, only academic papers. That means low competition, but the demand is unproven until interviews confirm it.

Talks and hiring posts point the same way. At the PX4 Developer Summit in October 2026, Heimdall Power named **"RTK degradation resulting in sudden position jumps"** as an open problem that it handles in its own software ([Sched abstract](https://osselceu2026.sched.com/event/2Rabx)). RTK is centimetre-level GPS. Delivery makers hire for intermittent GNSS (satellite positioning: GPS and its cousins), "onboard integrity tests" and "contingency management" ([Zipline](https://jobs.lererhippeau.com/companies/zipline/jobs/32391430-senior-gnc-engineer), [Wing](https://www.builtinboston.com/job/navigation-engineer/9274712)). None of the posts read asks for RL. Only Matternet names PX4 or ArduPilot ([Matternet](https://www.matternet.com/careers/ddv84u129ua2g09y9g09w2gxq4y12q)). The big teams (Zipline, Wing, Skydio) build their own stacks and are unlikely buyers. Judging by their job posts, the likelier buyers have thin GNC (guidance, navigation and control) teams, like Heimdall and Voliro, or buy their airframes, such as Gather AI on ModalAI's Starling 2 ([Gather AI](https://gather.ai/news-and-insights/gather-ai-leverages-new-modalai-r-starling-2-logis-drones-for-greater-warehouse-and-inventory-data-capabilities-1)).

The US Department of Homeland Security's urban test shows the stakes. In Manhattan the PX4-based Freefly Astro "would not enter position mode" and "drifted constantly", while Skydio stayed stable ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/09/25/dhs-blue-uas-report-three-of-five-grounded-nyc/)). A DJI Matrice 30T lost vision positioning at night and drifted into a structure ([DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2026/10/01/dhs-addendum-dji-matrice-scores-blue-uas-hudson-yards/)). Both reports are second-hand: the DHS report itself was not read. Be honest about what this shows. The Astro lacked a non-GPS position source, and Structure Hold does not make one.

## Buyers will judge a learned controller by how it fails

No regulator says "the drone must navigate without GPS". They all ask for containment. No single failure, a navigation failure included, may take the drone outside its operating area. Failures of those systems must be shown to the pilot. Above low robustness, any software that could cause a fly-out must be built to an accepted standard ([UK CAA, Annex E](https://regulatorylibrary.caa.co.uk/2019-947/Content/Article%2011/AMC1%20Article%2011%20Annex%20E.%20Containment%20requirements.htm); [Transport Canada Standard 922](https://tc.canada.ca/en/corporate-services/acts-regulations/list-regulations/canadian-aviation-regulations-sor-96-433/standards/standard-922-rpas-safety-assurance)). Canada's 922.08 says **"any failure of a system whose operation is required ... shall be annunciated to the operator"**. The UK lists "a fail-safe health monitoring system which is triggered in the event of a critical feature failure" as an acceptable means, and it names navigation as the example. That describes our watchdog. ASTM F3201 is the practical software standard for small drones. It names GNC software suppliers as users and accepts simulation, hardware-in-the-loop and flight tests as evidence ([ASTM F3201-26](https://store.astm.org/f3201-26.html)).

There are no binding machine-learning rules for small drones yet. EASA's first AI proposal was out for consultation in late 2025 ([EASA](https://easa.europa.eu/en/newsroom-and-events/news/easas-first-regulatory-proposal-artificial-intelligence-aviation-now-open)). I found no civilian company that has shipped an RL flight controller. PX4's docs list a neural-network control mode, but only the title was seen ([PX4 docs](https://docs.px4.io/main/en/neural_networks/mc_neural_network_control)). NXP's 2026 summit talk was about the friction of training RL against PX4, not about field results ([Sched](https://osselceu2026.sched.com/list/descriptions/type/PX4+Dev+Summit)). So lead every pitch with the classical watchdog. The learned part should look bounded, monitored and backed up.

Paperwork is growing too. The FCC's Covered List now includes the "associated software" of flight controllers and navigation systems ([Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/fcc-adds-all-foreign-made-uas-and-uas-critical-components-to-covered-list)). The EU Cyber Resilience Act requires vulnerability reporting from 11 September 2026 ([McCann FitzGerald](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/data-privacy-and-cyber-risk/cyber-resilience-act-reporting-obligations-apply-from-11-september-2026)). From 9 December 2026, EU product liability law treats software as a product ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/when-commercial-drones-go-to-work-out-of-sight-not-out-of-scope/1841254)). Insurer evidence is thin. One insurer asks for operations manuals and "redundancy in critical systems" ([SkyWatch](https://www.skywatch.ai/blog/beyond-visual-line-of-sight-bvlos-preparing-your-drone-business-for-advanced-operations)). Indoor and confined-space flights mostly sit outside aviation rules. There the site owner's safety rules decide ([British Safety Council](https://www.britsafe.org/safety-management/2025/drones-flying-high-or-greater-depths)).

## Structure Hold answers the top pain, but only in simulation

The "what we have" column below comes from the repository. The evidence levels are **GAZEBO** (PX4 v1.17.0 SITL against tuned stock PX4), **SIH** (PX4's built-in simulator, used for plumbing checks only), **MOCK** (our own batched simulator, compared against a simple cascaded PD controller called the "PD yardstick") and **REAL** (none yet).

| Real problem engineers report | Our answer today | Evidence level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lurch or drift when the estimate jumps, lags or drops out | Structure Hold v22. Mean p95 0.19 to 0.20 m against tuned PX4's 0.42 to 0.43 m at calm, 4 and 8 m/s, 0 crashes in 24 holds (`docs/baseline.md`) | GAZEBO: one airframe (x500), one flat wall, modelled VIO and wind |
| Blunt failsafes: blind land, land at the origin, drifting into a structure | Classical watchdog, then Structure Hover and Structure Land guarded by a forward rangefinder. Closest approach to the wall 0.65 to 0.69 m, against 0.21 to 0.33 m under stock Land | GAZEBO, one seed; SIH; C++ unit tests |
| Stock controller cannot hold in wind on a non-GPS source | Learned body-rate and thrust policy tested up to 8 m/s | GAZEBO, wind straight into the wall |
| Forward rangefinders are not first-class in stock firmware | Wall guard (released). v24 reads the range as a policy input (candidate, not released) | GAZEBO one seed (guard); MOCK (v24) |
| Firmware regressions across releases | Pinned to v1.17.0, unit tests, SIH acceptance runs | Helps, but also limits who can use it |
| Hard estimate integration | Frames in one file, ODOMETRY bridge, bench test, logging checklist | Simulation only; not packaged for an OEM (original equipment manufacturer: the drone maker) |
| Must fit small flight controllers with no companion computer | Compiled-in MLP; builds for Pixhawk 6C/6X at 96 % and 99.7 % flash | Compiled only; never flashed. Inference time estimated, not measured |
| Tunnels and corridors | Route Follow v0: cross-track p95 22 to 38 % tighter than the PD yardsticks | MOCK only |
| A safety review for a learned controller | Watchdog outside the learned part, handback order pinned by unit tests, parameter clamps, ground-engage refusal | SITL and unit tests; no FMEA or safety manual |

Three honest limits shape the pitch. First, most of the Gazebo gain comes on seeds where tuned PX4 loses the hold after an estimate fault, and seeds 4 and 8 are ties. So the claim is "robust to bad estimates", not "tighter in clean air". Second, the partner pack is unsent, and the Isaac Lab port has never run because there is no GPU server. Third, the top-level `README.md` still says "camera and IMU" and "deployed and flown on a small real drone". That contradicts both the scope and the evidence, and needs fixing before any outreach.

## What to build next

**First: the field-failure test pack with ground-station trip warnings (gaps 1 and 2), feeding the FMEA (gap 4).** This has the strongest buyer pull and sits on our core claim. It turns "robust to bad estimates" into a table an engineer can check: what happens when your VIO resets to zero, when the companion link dies, or when RTK jumps. Add the profiles to `envs/estimate` and the estimate bridge. Fly them in the mock and in SIH, against tuned PX4 as the benchmark rule requires. Send a PX4 event to the ground station on every trip and every wall-guard push. Each result becomes one FMEA row with its test reference.

**Second: interviews and the partner paperwork (gaps 3 and 6).** These are Zain's job, and they are the only route to a real flight (gap 11), which every buyer will ask for. Use the output of the first item as the outreach material. Start with Heimdall Power, whose public problem (sudden RTK jumps while working close to a structure) is close to ours. Fix the README before the first email.

**Third: a partner onboarding kit (gaps 5, 7 and 9).** That means the log health check (which doubles as a free tool for outreach), then RTK scoring, then the airframe fit tool. Together they make the partner pack's step 0 (logging flight) and step 6 (scoring) work on a real drone outdoors. All of it runs on CPU.

**Safe to start in code right now: the first item.** Begin with the profiles that need no GPS: reset to origin, VIO restart, link loss over 5 s, slow drift, and a frozen-but-valid estimate. Add the ground-station warning alongside them. This changes no Decided row. The new profiles sit beside the Decided corruption defaults, the warnings add messages without touching the handback order, and every result is still compared against tuned PX4. Hold the GPS-on transition profile until Zain answers whether partners may fly with GPS on.

The research changes the pitch more than the roadmap. Buyers will not pay for a learned controller because it is learned. They might pay for one that has a documented, tested answer to each way their estimate fails, and that hands control back in a way their regulator recognises. The 5 m passes and Route Follow are real opportunities, but building them before a first real flight would spread a small team across three unproven claims instead of proving one.
